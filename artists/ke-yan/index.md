The Godforsaken Acre is a 3D digital art project that reimagines classical romanticist landscape painting through the lens of dark fantasy, biopunk aesthetics, and contemporary real-time rendering workflows. Drawing direct inspiration from John Martin’s 1831 masterpiece The Fall of Babylon, this work seeks to translate the dramatic grandiosity, apocalyptic scale, and emotional weight of 19th-century oil painting into a fully realized three-dimensional digital space.

The core artistic narrative explores themes of fallen monumental structures, biological decay, and the visceral tension between organic anatomies and architectural decay. Rather than merely recreating a static image, the project expands the original composition into an atmospheric, storytelling-driven narrative space. Massive ruinous architecture is integrated with grotesque biopunk motifs, where twisted anatomical forms and alien flesh-like textures overtake ancient stone, evoking a sense of forgotten history and existential dread.

## About the Artist

Ke Yan is a Los Angeles-based 3D character and creature freelance artist. Specializing in dark fantasy, biopunk aesthetics, and intricate anatomical sculpting, his work merges classical artistic sensibilities with cutting-edge real-time technologies.

Ke utilizes an end-to-end digital pipeline incorporating ZBrush, Maya and Unreal Engine to construct atmospheric, narrative-driven assets and speculative worlds. His creative vision focuses on the interplay between organic decay, dark fantacy forms, and surreal horror, aiming to push the visual and emotional boundaries of modern digital storytelling across games, music visuals, and interactive media.
